# Hook formulas

Twelve shapes. Rotate them — using one shape repeatedly trains the audience to
scroll past it. Each entry gives the mechanism, a filled example, and the failure
mode that makes it fall flat.

Fill every bracket with something real from `brand/brand-config.md` → `## Proof`.
An unfilled bracket is not a hook.

---

**1. The cost.** Name what it cost before saying what it was.
> "That integration cost me ₹40,000 and a client before I understood the pricing page."

Mechanism: a concrete loss creates immediate stakes.
Fails when: the number is invented or rounded to look big.

---

**2. The reversal.** State what you believed, then that you were wrong.
> "I spent two years optimising my posting schedule. The schedule was never the problem."

Mechanism: an admission buys the reader's trust before the argument starts.
Fails when: the reversal is fake humility about something you never believed.

---

**3. The specific artefact.** Open on a thing, not a topic.
> "There is one line in my deploy script that has saved me four outages."

Mechanism: objects are concrete; concepts are not.
Fails when: the artefact turns out to be a metaphor.

---

**4. The count.** A number of attempts, versions, or failures.
> "I rewrote this onboarding email nine times. Version seven is the one that worked."

Mechanism: implies work done and a lesson extracted.
Fails when: the count is decorative and never referenced again.

---

**5. The overheard objection.** Quote what someone actually said to you.
> "A recruiter told me my portfolio 'looked like a template'. She was right."

Mechanism: dialogue reads as real and invites disagreement.
Fails when: the quote is invented. Never fabricate a quote.

---

**6. The before / after with a mechanism.** Both states plus the lever.
> "Two replies a week, then eleven. The only thing I changed was who I sent it to."

Mechanism: the gap plus a hint of causation.
Fails when: there is no real mechanism and it becomes a growth-hack claim.

---

**7. The consensus break.** Name what everyone says, then refuse it.
> "Everyone says to post daily. I post twice a week and reach more people than I did daily."

Mechanism: takes a side, which is what earns comments.
Fails when: the consensus is a straw man nobody actually holds.

---

**8. The thing you almost did.** The decision that nearly went the other way.
> "I nearly deleted the account in March. Here is the post that made me keep it."

Mechanism: near-misses carry the tension of a real choice.
Fails when: the stakes are inflated beyond what happened.

---

**9. The unglamorous detail.** The boring specific nobody mentions.
> "Nobody warns you that most of this job is renaming files."

Mechanism: recognition. The reader has felt it and never seen it written.
Fails when: the detail is generic rather than oddly specific.

---

**10. The deadline.** Time pressure that actually existed.
> "I had eleven hours to ship it and no design. This is what I cut."

Mechanism: a clock creates narrative pull.
Fails when: the deadline is invented for drama.

---

**11. The question you were asked twice.** A real repeated question.
> "Three people this month asked me the same thing about pricing. Here is the answer I give."

Mechanism: signals the content answers a live need.
Fails when: nobody actually asked.

---

**12. The receipt.** Open on the evidence itself.
> "My analytics for last month: 47,000 views, 1,200 saves, two enquiries. The gap is the whole lesson."

Mechanism: data first, interpretation second.
Fails when: the operator has not supplied the numbers. Use `[NUMBER: ...]`.

---

## Shapes to avoid entirely

- "🧵 A thread on..." — announces structure, not substance
- "Let's be honest" / "I'll be real with you" — performed candour
- "This changed everything for me" — no information
- "Read this if you..." — instructs before earning it
- "Unpopular opinion:" — usually a popular opinion
- "I'm not saying X, but..." — you are saying X
- A single word, then a full stop, then a line break — the LinkedIn tic
- Any question the post then answers in the next line — just make the claim

## Platform trims

The same hook, cut to fit — not rewritten from scratch:

| surface | trim |
|---|---|
| Reel | say it out loud; if you run out of breath, cut a clause |
| Carousel cover | under 9 words, one accent word, breaks on a sense unit |
| LinkedIn | complete thought inside ~140 characters |
| X | must work as a standalone post |
| Pulse title | drop the first-person framing, keep the specific noun |
